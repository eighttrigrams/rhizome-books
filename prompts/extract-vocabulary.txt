FOCUS, AND PAGE BOUNDARIES

Alongside the CURRENT page you are given the previous and the next page, when
those exist. They serve TWO purposes: a sentence containing an underlined word may
run across a page boundary, so the CURRENT page alone does not show all of it —
then read into the neighbour so you can quote the sentence in full. And the
CONTEXT line (see below) may draw on them.

Underlines are looked for on the CURRENT page ONLY. Never report a word because it
is underlined on a neighbouring page; that word belongs to that page's own run.

Repetition across runs is expected and is not a problem — do not try to avoid it.
If a sentence spans p.3 and p.4 and carries one underlined word on each page, then
the run for p.3 reports that sentence with only its p.3 word, and the run for p.4
reports the same sentence again with only its p.4 word. Stating the sentence twice
is correct. Each run focuses on its own page.

CONTEXT

The reader goes through these entries much later, away from the book, and then
often no longer knows what the passage was about: who "ele" or "ela" is, what
"isso" refers to, which scene or argument is going on. The CONTEXT line is there
to give back a picture of the situation the sentence stands in.

For each sentence, write a short summary, in German, of what is going on around
it: 1 or 2 sentences of your own, drawn from the CURRENT page and its neighbours.
Paint the situation: where we are, who is there, what just happened or is being
discussed, the mood of the scene or the drift of the argument — whatever lights
up the sentence. Name what the pronouns and references point to where that is
not obvious, but do not stop there; a little more of the surroundings is welcome
when it helps the reader see the scene.

Never repeat or paraphrase the sentence itself: the reader has it, and its
translation, right above. Tell what lies around it, not what it says. Nor
explain what the sentence and its translation already make plain — no glosses
like "the wall of silence is his silence". Explain a reference only when the
reader could really not tell what it points to without the surroundings.
Do not summarise the whole page either — only what helps understand this sentence.
